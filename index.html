How to measure

Blood pressure and pulse

Use the mission-approved cuff and supported position. Rest quietly first, support the arm at heart level, and record the repeat reading separately. For resting pulse, measure when calm and at rest.

Glucose and temperature

Use mission-approved devices and follow their instructions. Record glucose timing relative to food and the method used to measure temperature.

Sleep and mood

Enter time actually asleep during the last 24 hours, including naps. Choose the mood description that best fits today.

    Five hazards of human spaceflight
    

    Space radiation
    Inspect instrument readings in the NASA replay. In a mission, check personal dosimetry and follow radiation alerts and shelter procedures.
    

    Isolation and confinement
    Use sleep and mood logs to surface changes. Share sustained changes with crew medical support and use the mission’s fatigue and behavioral health procedures.
    

    Distance from Earth
    Record symptoms, readings, and steps taken in a briefing that can be stored locally when the link is unavailable.
    

    Gravity fields
    Changes in gravity can affect circulation and balance. Document dizziness and check whether a measurement followed exercise or a gravity transition.
    

    Hostile closed environments
    If multiple crew members feel unwell, follow the vehicle’s environmental checks. Cabin sensors provide CO₂, oxygen and pressure measurements.
